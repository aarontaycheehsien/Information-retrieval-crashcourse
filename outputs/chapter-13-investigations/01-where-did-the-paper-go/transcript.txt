Where did the paper go?

[00:00:00.700]: A paper you know is relevant is missing from the results. Did the search misunderstand your words? Before adding a synonym or replacing the model, pause. The same screen could hide several different failures. What evidence would distinguish them?

[00:00:18.044]: The chapter gives four alternative explanations: outside the collection, excluded by a filter, absent from the candidate pool, or retrieved but below the display boundary. Our diagrams show possible paths for the same hypothetical paper, not a traced search.

[00:00:36.581]: Start with eligibility. Was the source covered, and had indexing caught up? Did a date condition, format facet or access filter exclude the record? A paper outside the searchable collection or eligible set was never in play. Vocabulary expansion cannot recover it through that unchanged boundary.

[00:00:57.983]: Inspect coverage, limits and the record’s metadata. Some evidence is visible; some needs documentation. Record what remains unknown. An unavailable internal trace is not proof that the record was excluded.

[00:01:13.867]: Now separate the candidate pool from the displayed results. An eligible paper can fail to enter the first stage shortlist. Investigate the query, indexed evidence, retrieval method and candidate cutoff. A later reranker cannot assess a paper it never received.

[00:01:32.503]: A different paper may reach the pool and then fall below a later ranking or presentation limit. That is matched but hidden. Filter order and cutoff order can also change which records survive. A failed lookup alone does not identify the responsible boundary.

[00:01:50.903]: Check the indexed unit too. If the system ranks passages, a relevant claim split across two passages may leave neither unit with enough evidence. The whole document can contain the answer while each searchable unit carries only part of it.

[00:02:06.903]: And a typed word missing from the visible excerpt is a different question from a missing record. A match may occur in another indexed field or version. Revisit analysis and matching explanations before deciding what the interface has proved.

[00:02:23.128]: Once eligibility is established, the chapter offers four diagnostic lenses. Vocabulary mismatch compares a query’s language with a relevant record. Out of vocabulary is relative to a named vocabulary. Out of distribution compares training with deployment. Composition asks whether matching preserved the required relationship.

[00:02:46.275]: These are prompts for investigation, not exhaustive or mutually exclusive boxes. A specialised identifier can be poorly represented inside an unfamiliar corpus. One paper can support several hypotheses, which require different evidence and different remedies.

[00:03:05.334]: The source example searches for heart attack recovery, while the relevant title is Rehabilitation after myocardial infarction. That visible relationship is vocabulary mismatch. Neither expression is necessarily absent from the index or model vocabulary. Different wording alone does not establish out of vocabulary.

[00:03:27.267]: Librarians already build bridges with synonyms, controlled vocabulary, spelling variants, citation searching and iterative reformulation. Boolean OR can make an alternative term explicit. Query transformation can add a bridge automatically, but the generated input still needs checking.

[00:03:47.359]: BM twenty five changes ranking, not term identity. It can use partial lexical overlap, and analysis may connect morphological variants. Its formula does not itself make heart attack synonymous with myocardial infarction. Do not credit the scoring rule for an expansion performed elsewhere.

[00:04:07.221]: A suitably trained dense retriever may connect the complete query and document without exact overlap. That is a newer response to an old problem, not a guarantee for every paraphrase. Compare the suspected bridge against known relevant records; hybrid retrieval is another remedy to test.

[00:04:28.413]: Preserve the original query, known relevant record, visible limits, indexed-unit information and any available trace. Separate what you observed from what you inferred. Then choose an intervention that could test the suspected loss of evidence.

[00:04:44.955]: For a vocabulary mismatch, try an explicit synonym or controlled term and check the outcome. For an unknown candidate boundary, seek documentation or a trace first.
