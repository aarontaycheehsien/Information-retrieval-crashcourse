[00:01:48.623]: Explicit retrieval feedback uses the searcher’s judgements about accepted or rejected records to help construct another retrieval input. Pseudo relevance feedback asks the searcher for nothing: it assumes highly ranked records from an initial search are relevant.

[00:02:05.537]: It selects useful looking terms from those results and constructs a second query. If the first ranking is off topic, the added terms can reinforce that mistake. The second pass may add candidates, but the relevance assumption was unverified.

[00:02:22.489]: Active learning screening in systematic reviews also uses inclusion and exclusion judgements. But it repeatedly reprioritises the records already in an existing candidate pool. Those judgements do not inherently construct another retrieval pass or add records.

[00:02:51.804]: Pseudo relevance feedback needs an initial ranking before it can build a later query. It is therefore a retrieval to transformation loop, not a step that happens entirely before searching. Yet a designer can prescribe exactly those two passes every time.
