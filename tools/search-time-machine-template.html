    What is real here

    The collection is the prose of the book: __DOC_COUNT__ sections, their paragraphs, their cross-references and the __TERM_COUNT__ glossary terms. Each decade's list is computed live by a working implementation of that decade's method. Nothing is scripted, so your own queries work.

    • 1960s. The glossary serves as the thesaurus. An indexing pass assigns up to ten terms per section, and a request is answered only through those terms.
    • 1970s. Exact-word Boolean over an inverted file. Type AND, OR, NOT, "quoted phrases", parentheses or truncation (librar*) and it obeys.
    • 1980s. Keyword search over catalogue fields only: section title, chapter title and subject headings.
    • 1990s. Porter-stemmed tf-idf with cosine normalisation.
    • 2000s. BM25 with a title weight, a PageRank prior from the book's own cross-references, query-biased snippets and edit-distance spelling suggestions.
    • 2010s. Each paragraph is the weighted mean of its word vectors. Sections are ranked by cosine similarity to the query vector.
    • 2020s. BM25 and vector lists fused by reciprocal rank fusion, reranked by a soft word-matching score, then answered with quoted sentences.

    What is not

    The screens imitate the feel of each period. They are not replicas of any product, and their brand names are invented.

    The rank chips above are one tracked section, not an evaluation. By default the tracked section is whatever the 2020s pipeline put first, which flatters the 2020s. Click any result in any decade to follow that section instead, and try the Boolean example: the oldest online method is the only one that honours NOT.

    The vectors are 2014-era static word vectors, one per word, with book-specific terms placed by their context in the book. Trained dense retrievers and neural rerankers are far stronger and far too large to run in a page. The 2020s answer is stitched from quoted sentences, so it cannot invent anything and it cannot synthesise anything either.
