1 · What retrieval receives

Understanding and transformation

A system may interpret the request, then correct, map, expand or restructure it.

Optional. This changes the words sent on.

Analysis

Tokenise and normalise; perhaps stem and remove stop-words.

Routine. The output is the analysed query terms.

2 · Admission: which records may compete

All terms required

A Boolean AND over every term.

Some terms required

Minimum match, such as two of three.

Any term suffices

The union of the posting lists. BM25 commonly runs this way.

3 · Ranking: how candidates are ordered

BM25’s lexical evidence

Term rarity, term frequency with diminishing returns, and document length.

Further lexical evidence

Field weights, and phrase and proximity boosts.

Signals that are not lexical matches

Citations, recency, resource type or peer-review status can supplement the score.

4 · Output boundary: how many are kept

The engine may completely order the scored candidates or retain only a top-k. A later stage may rerank that shortlist.

Every admission rule here is lexical. Boolean AND is one way to control admission; minimum-match and any-match rules are others, and graded scoring orders whatever is admitted.
