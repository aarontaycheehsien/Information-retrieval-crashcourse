## More borderline, but worth highlighting

7. **“This does not make Boolean retrieval inferior: explicit eligibility rules remain valuable when a task requires inspectable, reproducible control over who may enter the result set.”** — [Line 1378](https://github.com/aarontaycheehsien/Information-retrieval-crashcourse/blob/fable-1/search-textbook.html#L1378)

   Unprompted reassurance, followed by a dense cluster of abstract virtues: “explicit,” “inspectable,” “reproducible.”

8. **“Text becomes tokens; analysis turns selected tokens into searchable terms.”** — [Line 1426](https://github.com/aarontaycheehsien/Information-retrieval-crashcourse/blob/fable-1/search-textbook.html#L1426)

   Another carefully balanced takeaway sentence. Reasonable individually; the repeated slogan format creates the AI-like impression.

9. **“Lemmatisation uses more linguistic information than stemming, but it is not automatically correct, semantic or superior for every collection.”** — [Line 1438](https://github.com/aarontaycheehsien/Information-retrieval-crashcourse/blob/fable-1/search-textbook.html#L1438)

   The three-part caveat has the exhaustive, qualification-heavy cadence common in LLM explanations.

10. **“That is a question about coverage. FTS is a description of a search capability.”** — [Line 1506](https://github.com/aarontaycheehsien/Information-retrieval-crashcourse/blob/fable-1/search-textbook.html#L1506)

   Useful distinction, but expressed as another tightly choreographed pair of corrective sentences.

The most noticeable pattern is repeated polished contrasts and takeaway slogans, particularly around “admission” versus “ranking.” The concrete examples and step-by-step index explanations draw much less attention.
