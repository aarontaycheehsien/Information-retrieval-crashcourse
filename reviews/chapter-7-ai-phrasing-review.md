These are passages flagged as sounding like “Claude speak”—a stylistic judgment, not evidence of authorship. This record preserves the review only; the chapter text has not been changed.

## Strongest examples

1. **“This chapter deploys that encoder.”** — [Line 2007](https://github.com/aarontaycheehsien/Information-retrieval-crashcourse/blob/fable-1/search-textbook.html#L2007)

   A strained metaphor that gives the chapter itself a technical action.

2. **“The second—that a vector need not be dense, learnt or semantic—is the next chapter’s opening business.”** — [Line 2007](https://github.com/aarontaycheehsien/Information-retrieval-crashcourse/blob/fable-1/search-textbook.html#L2007)

   Elaborate chapter choreography; “opening business” sounds conspicuously literary.

3. **“Human vision stops at three spatial dimensions; the arithmetic does not.”** — [Line 2022](https://github.com/aarontaycheehsien/Information-retrieval-crashcourse/blob/fable-1/search-textbook.html#L2022)

   A polished, symmetrical aphorism that sounds composed for effect.

4. **“The word semantic does not tell us which one, or where in the pipeline meaning enters.”** — [Line 2030](https://github.com/aarontaycheehsien/Information-retrieval-crashcourse/blob/fable-1/search-textbook.html#L2030)

   Abstractly personifies “meaning.” The pipeline phrasing recurs as a refrain in the figure and later discussion.

5. **“Encoding each passage before any query arrives buys one thing and costs another.”** — [Line 2044](https://github.com/aarontaycheehsien/Information-retrieval-crashcourse/blob/fable-1/search-textbook.html#L2044)

   A formulaic benefit-versus-cost setup that delays naming either one.

6. **“Topic, named entities, claims, qualifications and terminology are all squeezed into one point before the passage knows which query will arrive.”** — [Line 2064](https://github.com/aarontaycheehsien/Information-retrieval-crashcourse/blob/fable-1/search-textbook.html#L2064)

   The passage is personified as knowing things; the long list and metaphor make the sentence feel overworked.

7. **“Nearest means best available, not necessarily relevant.”** — [Line 2079](https://github.com/aarontaycheehsien/Information-retrieval-crashcourse/blob/fable-1/search-textbook.html#L2079)

   A standalone slogan built around a corrective contrast. It contributes to the repeated aphoristic cadence.

8. **“k says how many leaders were requested—not who could compete, how relevance was calculated or how much work was done.”** — [Line 2129](https://github.com/aarontaycheehsien/Information-retrieval-crashcourse/blob/fable-1/search-textbook.html#L2129)

   A tidy three-part contrast and extended competition metaphor, restating distinctions already explained.

9. **“Puzzle 3 set a name against two result counts and an inspection of search quality.”** — [Line 2134](https://github.com/aarontaycheehsien/Information-retrieval-crashcourse/blob/fable-1/search-textbook.html#L2134)

   An abstract, unnatural description of a straightforward comparison; “set a name against” feels rhetorically manufactured.

10. **“This is the strongest documented explanation for why the dense-retrieval intuition is unsafe, but it is not proof of the exact August 2026 cause.”** — [Line 2138](https://github.com/aarontaycheehsien/Information-retrieval-crashcourse/blob/fable-1/search-textbook.html#L2138)

    Heavy qualification around the awkward abstraction “the dense-retrieval intuition is unsafe.” The following sentences repeat the caution.

11. **“OpenAlex Alice semantic search supplies a deliberately stark contrast in which the documentation does identify dense first-stage retrieval.”** — [Line 2140](https://github.com/aarontaycheehsien/Information-retrieval-crashcourse/blob/fable-1/search-textbook.html#L2140)

    Announces how emphatic the comparison should feel instead of letting the evidence establish it.

12. **“The search box tells you how the user can express the query. ‘Semantic search’ tells you what kind of matching the system aspires to provide. Dense retrieval, lexical retrieval and reranking tell you how particular stages may actually do the work.”** — [Line 2156](https://github.com/aarontaycheehsien/Information-retrieval-crashcourse/blob/fable-1/search-textbook.html#L2156)

    A very neat parallel construction that repackages the chapter’s lesson as a rehearsed closing speech.

13. **“Similarity is a comparison rule applied to stored vectors, not a verdict about them.”** — [Line 2157](https://github.com/aarontaycheehsien/Information-retrieval-crashcourse/blob/fable-1/search-textbook.html#L2157)

    “Not a verdict” adds a dramatic metaphor to another corrective slogan.

14. **“Both routes compared stored representations without ever asking what one of those representations contains, or what a single stored item stands for in the collection a reader believes they are searching. The next chapter opens the stored object itself.”** — [Line 2157](https://github.com/aarontaycheehsien/Information-retrieval-crashcourse/blob/fable-1/search-textbook.html#L2157)

    The strongest example: abstract, inflated setup followed by a theatrical teaser. “Opens the stored object itself” is especially conspicuous.
